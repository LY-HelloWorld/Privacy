Family sharing guide

Keep the family moment without publishing every face

Group photos can include your child, another family's child, a foster child, or a bystander whose face should not be posted publicly.

The real tradeoff

Parents often want a meaningful family photo without creating a permanent public identity trail for a child. Cropping may remove the moment; a large emoji can dominate it; leaving the face visible may cross a family boundary.

A safer preparation checklist

  1. Confirm that you have permission to share the photo.
  2. Review every face, including people entering during Live Photo motion.
  3. Adjust masks so they remain effective without covering more of the scene than needed.
  4. Mute names or private conversation captured in the audio.
  5. Remove source location metadata and inspect visible school, home, and event clues.
  6. Share only with the audience you intended.

Consent still comes first

Blurring does not automatically make publication appropriate. A child's body, clothing, voice, location, or context may still be identifying or private. When in doubt, keep the photo offline or share it directly with a limited audience.
